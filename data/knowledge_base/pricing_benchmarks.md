# Pricing Benchmarks and Cost Intelligence

## Market Benchmark Methodology

Pricing benchmarks are updated quarterly using a blend of: publicly available tender data, industry indices (e.g., IEEMA for electrical equipment, CII for industrial MRO), historical internal spend data, and third-party market intelligence feeds. A supplier's cost score reflects deviation from the category benchmark.

Benchmark delta scoring:
  - More than 8% below benchmark: 10/10 (exceptional)
  - 5–8% below benchmark: 8–9
  - 1–4% below benchmark: 7–8
  - Within ±1% of benchmark: 6–7 (market-rate)
  - 1–4% above benchmark: 4–5
  - 5–8% above benchmark: 2–3
  - More than 8% above benchmark: 1 (disqualifying for cost-sensitive categories)

## Category Benchmarks (Q1 2025)

MRO — Industrial Fasteners and Safety Equipment: baseline $18.40/unit (mixed SKU average). Preferred suppliers average 4.1% below benchmark.

IT Hardware — Laptops and Workstations: baseline $62,000 per 10-unit lot (mid-range commercial grade). Market is volatile; benchmark updated monthly during supply shortages.

Electronic Components — PCB Assemblies: baseline $2,400/batch (standard 50-unit run). Component shortages in Q2–Q3 2024 pushed spot prices 12–18% above benchmark; normalized in Q4 2024.

IT Services — Managed Services and Software Licensing: baseline $4,200/user/year for standard ERP seat. Cloud infrastructure services benchmarked against AWS/Azure published rates.

Raw Materials — Structural Steel: baseline $58,500/MT (IS 2062 Grade A). Pricing indexed against LME steel futures; quarterly review mandatory.

Mechanical Components — Precision Machined Parts: baseline $3,800/unit (category average, complexity-adjusted). Suppliers at 3–5% below benchmark considered competitive.

Logistics — Last-Mile Indirect: baseline $180/consignment (metro delivery, <50kg). Fuel surcharges tracked separately; included in benchmark from Q2 2024.

## Volume Discount Expectations

Standard volume thresholds for negotiation: 10% discount at 200-unit minimum, 15% at 500-unit minimum, 20%+ at 1,000-unit minimum. Suppliers not offering volume discounts at these thresholds are flagged in cost benchmarking.

## Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Factors

Cost score must account for TCO where data is available: purchase price + estimated rework/rejection cost (based on historical defect rates) + logistics cost differential + payment terms discount. A supplier 3% above purchase-price benchmark but with zero rejections and net-60 payment terms may have a better TCO than a cheaper supplier with 2% rejection rates.
